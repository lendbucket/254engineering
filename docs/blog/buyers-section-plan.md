## Notes for the session that runs the build and audits

- Seven posts written, three folded. Titles are the operator's, unchanged.
- No prices are typed. The cost post links /structural-engineer/cost and /services/foundation-inspections. The only figure typed is the City of Denton's published foundation repair permit fee ($100), cited to the city's page.
- The registration renders through `registrationStatement()` (post 7), already imported in `insights.ts`. No engineer is named and no license, registration or appointment number is typed.
- Calls to action are inline links in body paragraphs to service routes that exist in `src/content/services.ts` (`foundation-inspections`, `roof-inspections`, `repair-specifications`, `structural-letters`) and to /structural-engineer/cost, following the windstorm branch's pattern: one after the opening, one mid post, one at the end. None uses order or booking language.
- Links to windstorm branch slugs: `windstorm-inspection-coastal-buyers` and `hurricane-damage-structural-checklist`. They 404 if this branch merges before `content/blog-windstorm`.
- The registry entries sit after the "structural engineer, proximity intent" topic, not at the end of the array, so they do not conflict with the windstorm branch's appended entries. Each topic name equals the post's `primaryKeyword`.
- The TREC contract text was read from the Justia Forms copy of the resale contract, and the edition and effective date from TREC's form 20-18 page. The termination option is described without a paragraph number, because paragraph numbering differs between editions.
- Sources the writing session could not open (blocked by the fetch tool): the Cornell LII copy of 22 TAC 535.228 and the TREC hydrostatic testing FAQ. The Justia copy of 535.228 was used; it states it is current through June 27, 2025, and the August 10, 2026 amendment is cited from TREC's rule notice.
