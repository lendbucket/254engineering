1. Purpose

This protocol establishes how 254 Engineering Services performs roof certifications of existing roofs
so that the engineer can issue a sealed certification letter supported by an evidence package
collected by a field technician under the engineer's supervision.

The engineer signs and seals the certification letter personally and the recipient relies on that
opinion. Every requirement in this protocol exists so that the engineer can reach a determination
from the evidence package alone.

2. Scope

This protocol applies to condition assessment and certification of existing roofs on residential and
light commercial structures where a client requires a professional opinion on the condition of the roof
for insurance binding or renewal, a property sale, lending, or a comparable purpose recorded at
intake.

This protocol does not cover windstorm inspections of ongoing construction, which are covered by
the firm's windstorm inspection protocol; forensic or cause-of-loss investigation; structural design;
plan review for permitting; or repair specification. Each of those is a separate service line under its
own protocol and engagement terms.

6. Intake

   · The coordinator opens a job file for each property. The file records the intake answers and
       customer uploads required by Appendix A.

   · The engineer records the purpose of the letter and the recipient exactly as given at intake. The
       letter is addressed only to that recipient and issued only for that purpose.

   · A yes to any flag question in Appendix A routes the job to the engineer before dispatch. The
       engineer records accept, accept with conditions, or decline.

   · A job is not dispatched until intake is complete and every upload required by Appendix A has
       been received.

   · The engineer records any condition of acceptance in the job file, including any item the
       technician must capture beyond Appendix B.

7. Scheduling and dispatch

   · The coordinator schedules an accepted job within two business days. The engineer may set a
       different interval for a job in the job file.

   · The technician confirms access, occupancy, and attic availability before travel.
   · Where site conditions prevent complete capture, including weather, access, safety, or roof

       steepness, the technician records each item that could not be observed and the reason. No
       item is estimated, assumed, or left blank.
   · Where the roof cannot be safely walked, the technician does not walk it. The engineer decides
       from the evidence collected whether a determination is possible or the job requires lift or
       equipment pricing.

8. Field inspection

The technician completes every item of Appendix B for the property, in the order listed, and follows
the photo procedure printed at the head of that checklist.

   · Before starting, the technician makes a rough sketch of the roof, marks North, and
       photographs the sketch.

   · The technician ticks the item being photographed, photographs the checklist, then takes the
       photographs for that item, and moves to the next item the same way.

   · An item that does not apply to the property is marked with the reason it does not apply.
   · The counts called for on the checklist are recorded as counts, not as descriptions.
   · The seal-bond check is performed and the ambient temperature at the time of the check is

       recorded, because the result is not meaningful without it.

9. Evidence standard and engineer review

Evidence package

                             254 Engineering Services | 254-RC-001 Roof Certification Protocol v1.0 | Page 3
Each job produces one evidence package in the firm's field application, consisting of the completed
Appendix B checklist, the photographs, and the technician's notes.

   · Every checklist item has at least one wide photograph placing the item on the structure and
       one close photograph showing its condition.

   · Where a dimension, exposure, or thickness governs, a ruler or tape is in frame.
   · The worst condition on each roof plane is photographed. A representative sample selected for

       appearance is not acceptable.
   · Every patch, prior repair, penetration, and flashing location is photographed individually.
   · Photographs carry automatic timestamp and location from the field application. Photographs

       from other devices are not accepted.
   · An observation that cannot be made is recorded as not observed with the reason.
   · A package that does not cover every checklist item, with the photographs each item calls for, is

       rejected without review.

Engineer review
   · The engineer reviews each evidence package and records one of the five determinations in
       Appendix C: pass, revise, repairs required, site revisit, or decline.
   · The engineer's review record identifies the checklist items and photographs relied on for the
       determination.
   · A package that does not allow the engineer to verify a required item is returned for revision or
       a site revisit. The customer is not charged for a re-inspection caused by an incomplete
       package.

10. Repairs and deficiencies

   · On a repairs required determination, the engineer issues a written repair list identifying each
       item, what governs it, and what is required to close it.

   · Repairs are performed by others. 254 Engineering Services does not perform, supervise, or
       recommend a contractor for the repair work.

   · Corrections are verified on a site revisit to the same evidence standard, and the engineer
       closes each item individually.

   · No certification letter is issued with an open repair item. There is no conditional certification.

11. Certification letter

   · On a pass determination, the engineer issues a sealed letter addressed to the recipient
       recorded at intake, for the purpose recorded at intake.

   · The letter identifies the property, the date of inspection, the covering type, the method by
       which the evidence was collected and reviewed, and the observed condition.

   · The letter states observed condition only. It does not estimate remaining service life, forecast
       future performance, or represent that the roof will not leak.

   · The letter states that the inspection was visual and non-destructive, that it reflects condition on
       the date of inspection only, and that no representation is made about concealed conditions.

   · The letter is not reassigned to another recipient, another purpose, or another date. A new
       inspection is required.

   · The engineer does not certify a roof that was not inspected under this protocol by this firm.

12. Exclusions

The following are declined at this service tier or routed to another service line under its own
engagement terms. The coordinator records the routing in the job file and informs the customer in
writing.

   · An open insurance claim on the roof, where a certification would function as claim leverage.
       Routed to the forensic line or declined.

   · Active or threatened litigation involving the roof.
   · A covering type outside the engineer's competency.
   · Unsafe access that prevents minimum capture where the customer refuses lift or equipment

       pricing.
   · A customer request for a predetermined conclusion. This is an automatic decline and the

       request is logged verbatim.

13. Records and retention

   · Each job file contains: the intake record and customer uploads, the engineer's acceptance
       record, the dispatch record, the completed Appendix B checklist, all photographs, the
       technician's notes, the engineer's review record and determination, any repair list and its
       closure records, and the issued letter.

   · Job files are retained for ten years from the date of the letter, or longer where required by
       TBPELS rules or the firm's professional liability insurance.

   · The compliance file holds the engineer's license records, technician qualification and training
       records, and every issued version of this protocol.

14. Protocol maintenance

   · The engineer reviews this protocol annually, and within thirty days of any change to the
       adopted code, to a governing manufacturer standard, or to the firm's field application.

   · Revisions are issued as a new version number with the effective date and the engineer's
       approval recorded in the document control table.

   · This document follows the format established by 254-WP-001 and carries the portal's four
       parts as Appendices A, B, and C.

Appendix C. Engineer decision rules

Applied by the engineer to each evidence package. One determination is recorded per review.

PASS
­ Package complete: every checklist item present or properly excepted
­ No active leak evidence (no daylight, no active moisture at stains)
­ Covering condition consistent with reported age; worst-area photos show serviceable covering per
the letter's stated criteria
­ Seal-bond, fastening, flashings, and penetrations show no condition requiring repair before
certification

REVISE
­ Any required photo missing, blurry, or missing its ruler/context pair
­ Worst-area coverage doubtful (all photos look selectively good)
­ Exception used where the condition plainly applied
­ Counts inconsistent with photos

REPAIRS REQUIRED (certification withheld, repair list issued)
­ Active leak: daylight through deck or active moisture at any stain
­ Deck delamination or sag confirmed by measurement
­ Failed flashing, boots, or sealant at any penetration
­ Unsealed tabs at more than 25% of tested locations
­ Covering damage beyond 10 units on any plane
­ 8 or more hail hits in a 10 by 10 test square, where a test square was taken on a site revisit
­ Certification proceeds only after repairs are verified on revisit

SITE REVISIT (technician returns with an engineer-specified capture list)
­ Attic coverage incomplete: hatch photo only, no plane-by-plane daylight and stain check
­ Stain present without a moisture reading -- return with meter and wet-test each stain
­ Sag or delamination suspected in sightline photos -- return for straightedge/string-line
measurement and framing spacing

                            254 Engineering Services | 254-RC-001 Roof Certification Protocol v1.0 | Page 10
­ Storm damage reported within 12 months -- return for test-square count on each plane (hail hits /
creased tabs)
­ Conflicting evidence (owner story vs photos vs uploads) -- return for the specific items that settle
it: product label, permit sticker, invoice match to covering
­ Prior adverse report exists -- return and photograph every condition the report cites at the same
locations before contradicting it
­ Repairs required -- return after repairs to verify each item on the repair list

DECLINE
­ Open insurance claim where the certification would function as claim leverage -- route to forensic
line under its own engagement terms, or decline
­ Active or threatened litigation -- decline at this service tier
­ Covering type outside engineer competency.
­ Unsafe access that prevents minimum capture and customer refuses lift/equipment pricing
­ Customer requests a predetermined conclusion -- automatic decline, log verbatim request
