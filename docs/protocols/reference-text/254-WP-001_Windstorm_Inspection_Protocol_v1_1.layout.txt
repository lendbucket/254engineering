1. Purpose

This protocol establishes how 254 Engineering Services performs windstorm inspections of ongoing
construction so that the Engineer of Record, as a Texas Department of Insurance appointed
qualified inspector, can certify compliance on Form WPI-2 and the owner can obtain a Certificate of
Compliance, Form WPI-8, from TDI. A WPI-8 is required for windstorm and hail coverage through
the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association on structures in the Designated Catastrophe Areas.

The engineer signs and seals the WPI-2 personally and TDI relies on that certification. Every
requirement in this protocol exists so that the engineer can reconstruct and defend each certification
from the job file alone.

2. Scope

This protocol applies to ongoing construction: new structures, additions, alterations, repairs, and re-
roofs for which the first inspection occurs before the structure becomes a completed improvement as
defined in Insurance Code Section 2210.251 and 28 TAC Chapter 5, Subchapter E. Completed
improvements are outside this protocol; Section 12 governs how such requests are handled.

This protocol does not cover structural design, plan review for permitting, or engineering letters
unrelated to the windstorm program. Those are separate engagements with their own protocols.

5. Personnel qualifications

Field Technician
   · At least two years of documented residential or light commercial construction experience in
       framing, roofing, or building envelope work, or an engineering degree with EIT certification, or
       equivalent experience the engineer accepts in writing.
   · Completion of the engineer's training on this protocol, Appendix A, and the field application,
       with a supervised inspection on at least two stages before working alone.
   · For drone operation, a current FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate on file.
   · Demonstrated ability to read plans and locate items in the field against them.
   · Annual refresher training when the adopted code, TDI rules, or this protocol change.

Engineer of Record
   · Active Texas P.E. license and active TDI appointment as a qualified inspector. Copies of both,
       with expiration dates, are kept in the firm's compliance file and checked monthly by the
       coordinator.

6. Intake and the WPI-1

  1. The coordinator opens a job file for each detached structure. The file records the owner,
       contractor, site address, county, structure type and occupancy, scope of work, construction
       documents received, construction start date, and the expected construction sequence.

  2. The engineer reviews the intake and records in the file: accept, accept with conditions, or
       decline. A structure that has already passed a stage that must be inspected is declined for the
       ongoing path and handled under Section 12.

  3. The engineer records the design criteria for the job: applicable code edition by construction
       start date, design wind speed and exposure from the TDI tools and code maps, windborne
       debris region status, and any Texas or TDI amendments that apply.

  4. The engineer reviews the design documents for the job as 28 TAC 5.4623 requires, including
       the design criteria, wind loads, plans, and building product specifications, before the first
       inspection.

  5. Form WPI-1, Application for Windstorm Building Inspection, is submitted to TDI before
       construction begins or as soon as possible after, naming the Engineer of Record as the
       appointed inspector. The firm files it through the engineer's TDI Windstorm system login. The
       engineer confirms it is on file in the TDI Windstorm system before the first inspection and
       records the application identifier in the job file.

6. The engineer issues a job inspection plan listing the required stages for the scope, drawn from
     Section 8 and Appendix A, and the coordinator delivers it to the contractor in writing with the
     notice requirements of Section 7.

7. The coordinator records the construction start date and the first inspection date. Where the
     interval between them is more than thirty days, the engineer documents the reason in the file
     and addresses it in the WPI-2 remarks, since TDI may require verification of that interval
     before issuing the certificate.

7. Scheduling and notice

   · The contractor must request each stage inspection before the work of that stage is covered.
       The coordinator confirms every inspection in writing to the contractor stating the stage, the
       date, and what must be complete, visible, and accessible.

   · The firm schedules a requested inspection within two business days of the request. The
       engineer may set a different interval for a specific job in the inspection plan.

   · Work covered before its stage inspection is not certified on the basis of the contractor's
       representation. The engineer determines whether the work can be exposed and inspected; if it
       cannot, the ongoing path for that structure is closed and the coordinator informs the owner and
       contractor in writing.

   · If site conditions prevent a complete inspection (access, safety, weather, work not ready), the
       technician records the condition, collects whatever evidence is available, and the coordinator
       reschedules. An incomplete stage is not closed.

9. Evidence standard and engineer review

Evidence package

Each stage produces one evidence package in the firm's field application, consisting of the
completed Appendix A checklist, photographs, measurements, and the technician's flagged
concerns. Packages are locked on submission and cannot be edited afterward.

   · Every checklist item has at least one wide photograph placing the item in the structure and one
       close photograph showing the detail with a tape or scale in frame.

   · Fastener spacing is photographed with a tape along the panel edge and across the field at
       each location where the checklist requires a measurement. Fastener heads must be
       identifiable as to type, size, seating, and whether they struck framing.

   · Every connector, strap, anchor, and hold-down is photographed with fastening visible and
       manufacturer identification legible.

   · Every product subject to a TDI product evaluation is photographed with its label, stamp, or
       rating legible, and the evaluation report number is recorded on the checklist.

   · Photographs are captured directly in the firm's field application and carry device time, server
       time, the difference between them, and location. Photographs from other devices are not
       accepted.

   · Minimum photograph counts per stage are set in Appendix B. A package below the minimum
       is rejected without review.

   · Observations that cannot be made are recorded as not observed with the reason. No item is
       estimated, assumed, or left blank.

   · Drone imagery is taken at stages 4 and 5 where airspace rules and weather allow, and
       consists of stills of every roof plane and a low-altitude pass along each eave and gable. Where
       a drone cannot be flown, every roof plane, eave, and gable is photographed from the roof, a
       ladder, or the ground, and the reason is recorded. Drone imagery supplements and does not
       replace close photographs of fastening.

Engineer review

   · The engineer reviews each package before the next stage is scheduled and records one of
       three determinations in the file with the date: stage closed, deficiency notice issued, or re-
       inspection ordered.

   · The engineer's review record identifies which checklist items and photographs the engineer
       relied on for the determination.

   · A package that does not allow the engineer to verify a required item is rejected and the stage
       is re-inspected. The technician's assessment is not a substitute for the evidence.

Engineer supervision
   · The engineer does not attend the site. Evidence is collected by the field technician as the
       engineer's designated representative, under the engineer's direct supervision.
   · The engineer is available to the technician by phone or live video during an inspection when
       the technician needs a decision, and may direct additional observations.

10. Deficiencies

  1. The engineer issues a written deficiency notice to the contractor and owner identifying each
       item, the governing requirement, the observation, the photographs, and the required
       correction.

  2. Corrections are re-inspected to the same evidence standard. The engineer reviews the re-
       inspection package and closes the deficiency in the file with the closing evidence and date.

  3. A deficiency concealed before re-inspection is treated as covered work under Section 7.
  4. No WPI-2 is submitted with an open deficiency. There is no conditional certification.

11. Certification

   · After the final stage is closed, the engineer completes Form WPI-2, Inspection Verification,
       listing every inspection date, the applicable code edition, and the certification of compliance,
       then signs and seals it. The coordinator may enter the inspection data in the TDI Windstorm
       system; the engineer reviews the entry and submits the signed and sealed form as TDI
       requires.

   · TDI issues the WPI-8. The coordinator retrieves it from the Windstorm system, delivers it to the
       owner and contractor with the job file summary, and closes the file.

   · The engineer does not certify a structure that was not inspected stage by stage under this
       protocol by this firm. Requests to certify work inspected by another engineer or firm are
       declined.

   · If TDI requests verification or additional information, the engineer responds from the job file.
       The coordinator tracks the request and the response with dates.

12. Completed improvements

A structure is a completed improvement when title has transferred from the builder to the initial
owner before the first inspection, or when the final framing stage, including attachment of component
and cladding items and installation of windborne debris protection, was completed before the first
inspection. TDI's published flowchart determines the path. Completed improvements obtain
certification through TWIA (Form WPI-3 and Certificate WPI-8-C) or through TDI's post-construction
process under Insurance Code Section 2210.2515 (Form WPI-2E and Certificate WPI-8E, supported
by a sealed post-construction inspection report or sealed design).

Completed improvements are certified under the firm's completed construction windstorm protocol,
not this one. The coordinator opens each such request as a job on that line.

13. Records and retention

   · Each job file contains: intake record and design criteria, WPI-1 confirmation and application
       identifier, job inspection plan, every evidence package, every engineer review record, every
       deficiency notice and closure, the submitted WPI-2, the issued WPI-8, and all correspondence
       with the owner, contractor, and TDI.

   · Job files are retained for ten years from the date of the WPI-8, or longer where required by
       TBPELS rules or a litigation hold. No record in a job file is altered or deleted.

   · The compliance file holds the engineer's license and appointment records, technician
       qualification records, training records, this protocol and every superseded version, and the
       annual protocol review record.

14. Protocol maintenance

   · The engineer reviews this protocol annually, and within thirty days of any change to the
       adopted code, TDI rules, TDI forms, or the TDI product evaluation program. The coordinator
       monitors the TDI Windstorm Inspection Program page and newsletters monthly and forwards
       changes to the engineer.

   · Revisions are issued as a new version number with the effective date and the engineer's
       approval recorded in the document control table. Jobs in progress continue under the version
       in force at their WPI-1 date unless the engineer directs otherwise in writing.

   · This document is the template for every other inspection and certification protocol the firm
       issues. Each follows the same structure: governing requirements, roles, qualifications, intake,
       stages, evidence standard, engineer review, deficiencies, certification, records, and
       maintenance.
