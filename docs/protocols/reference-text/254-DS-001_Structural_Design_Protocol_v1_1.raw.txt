1. Purpose
This protocol establishes how 254 Engineering Services performs structural design so that the
engineer can issue sealed drawings and calculations.
Design is the engineer's own original work. No evidence package arrives for review and no
determination is recorded. This protocol sets how a design engagement is scoped, what the
engineer designs from, what is checked before anything is sealed, and what is kept.
2. Scope
This protocol applies to structural design of residential and light commercial structures and elements:
new buildings, additions, alterations, beams and headers for wall removals, foundations, decks,
patio covers, carports, retaining walls, strengthening for rooftop solar, and engineered retrofits that
come out of another service line.
This protocol does not cover architectural, civil, mechanical, electrical, or plumbing design;
geotechnical investigation; design of components that are designed by their manufacturer, such as
trusses and metal buildings, beyond stating the loads and reviewing the submittal; or special
inspection and testing. The firm does not perform forensic or cause-of-loss investigation under this
or any protocol.
3. Governing requirements
· The building code adopted by the authority having jurisdiction and the standards it references.
The coordinator records the authority and the code edition at scoping. The engineer confirms
both and records them on the drawings.
· In the designated catastrophe area, the windstorm building code under the TWIA plan of
operation, where the owner will need a TDI certificate.
· Texas Engineering Practice Act and TBPELS rules, 22 TAC Chapter 137, including the sealing
rules.

4. Roles and responsibilities
Only the Engineer of Record decides whether an engagement is accepted, the design basis, and
what is issued. The coordinator relays the engineer's written decisions without interpretation or
addition.
· The engineer prepares the calculations. Drawings are drafted by the engineer or by a drafter
working under the engineer's direct supervision.
· The engineer does not attend the site. Site information comes from the client's documents and,
where the engineer asks for it, from a technician working to a list the engineer writes for that
job.
· The contractor is responsible for means and methods, temporary shoring, and site safety. The
drawings say so.
5. Personnel qualifications
Field Technician
· Qualified under one of the firm's inspection protocols, with a supervised inspection completed.
· Demonstrated ability to measure and sketch existing construction and to read plans.
Drafter
· Works only from the engineer's sketches, markups, and written instructions. Makes no design
decision.
Engineer of Record
· Active Texas P.E. license. A copy, with expiration date, is held in the firm's compliance file.
· The engineer accepts only work within the engineer's area of competence.
6. Intake
· The coordinator opens an engagement file for each request. The file records the scoping
answers and client documents required by Appendix A.
· Every request goes to the engineer. The engineer records accept, accept with conditions, or
decline, under Appendix C.
· On acceptance the engineer writes the design basis: what is being designed, what is not, the
code, the loads, the documents the design relies on, what will be delivered, and the delivery
date.
· The design basis is sent to the client in writing with the proposal. Design does not start until the
client accepts it in writing.
· Delivery is quoted for each engagement. No standard turnaround is published.
7. Scheduling and dispatch
· A technician is dispatched only where the engineer asks for site information, or the owner
orders construction observation, and the engineer writes the job list for it.
· The coordinator schedules the visit within two business days of the engineer's job list. The
engineer may set a different interval in the engagement file.

8. Site information
Where the engineer asks for a site visit, the technician completes every item of Appendix B and
every item on the engineer's job list, and follows the photo procedure printed at the head of that
checklist.
· Before starting, the technician makes a rough sketch of the site or structure, marks North, and
photographs the sketch.
· The technician ticks the item being photographed, photographs the checklist, then takes the
photographs for that item, and moves to the next item the same way.
· Existing construction is measured, not estimated. Every measurement has the tape in frame.
· Photographs are captured directly in the firm's field application and carry device time, server
time, the difference between them, and location.
· An observation that cannot be made is recorded as not observed with the reason. The
engineer states on the drawings every existing condition that was assumed and must be
verified by the contractor before work.
9. Design and engineer review
Design
· The engineer designs to the design basis. A change to the design basis is recorded in writing
and sent to the client before the design continues.
· Calculations show the code, the loads, the load path to the foundation, and each member and
connection designed.
· Drawings show the code and design loads, material specifications, every member and
connection designed, the existing conditions assumed, and what is to be designed by others.
Engineer review
· Before anything is sealed, the engineer completes the issue check in Appendix C and records
it in the engagement file.
· A drawing drafted by someone else is checked by the engineer line by line against the
calculations before it is sealed.
· The engineer checks his own work using the issue check. There is no second engineer.
10. Revisions and construction questions
· A document released before it is final is marked preliminary, not for construction, permit, or
bidding, with the engineer's name, license number, and the date. It is not sealed.
· A change after issue is made by a numbered revision, with the change marked and dated, and
sealed again. The superseded sheets stay in the engagement file.
· Plan review comments from the permitting authority are answered by the engineer in writing. A
comment that changes the design is a revision.
· Contractor questions and submittals for the firm's design are answered by the engineer in
writing, from the desk.
· Where the owner orders construction observation, a technician photographs the work at the
stages the engineer lists, to the firm's evidence standard, and the engineer reviews each
package and reports in writing what was observed. The engineer does not attend the site.

· A change in scope beyond the design basis is a new proposal.
11. Design deliverables
· The engineer seals, signs, and dates the final drawings, and the final calculations where the
permitting authority or the client requires them.
· Sealed documents are issued as PDF with the engineer's seal and signature as TBPELS rules
allow. Editable drawing files are not released.
· Each sheet carries the property, the engagement number, the revision, and the date.
· The drawings state that they are for the property and the scope named and are not to be used
for another site.
· The engineer seals only work prepared by the engineer or under the engineer's direct
supervision.
· The firm does not warrant construction and does not forecast future performance.
12. Exclusions
The following are declined. The coordinator records the decision in the engagement file and informs
the client in writing.
· Work outside residential and light commercial structures, or outside the engineer's area of
competence. Declined.
· A request to seal drawings or calculations prepared by others who were not under the
engineer's supervision. Declined.
· A request to design to a code or load less than the adopted code requires. Declined.
· A request tied to an open insurance claim or litigation over existing damage. Declined, with a
referral to an independent firm.
· A design that depends on soil information the client will not obtain, where the engineer finds
assumed values are not appropriate. Declined until a geotechnical report is supplied.
· A client request for a predetermined conclusion. This is an automatic decline and the request
is logged verbatim.
13. Records and retention
· Each engagement file contains: the scoping record and client documents, the engineer's
acceptance record, the design basis and the client's acceptance of it, any site information
package and job list, the calculations, the issue check, every issued document and every
revision, plan review comments and answers, contractor questions and answers, and any
construction observation packages and reports.
· Engagement files are retained for ten years from the date of the last sealed issue. For an
engagement that produces no sealed document, the ten years runs from the date of the
engineer's last recorded decision or closure.
· Ten years is the firm's floor, reviewed against TBPELS rules.
· The compliance file holds the engineer's license records, technician qualification and training
records, and every issued version of this protocol.
14. Protocol maintenance
· The engineer reviews this protocol annually, and within thirty days of any change to the
adopted code or to TBPELS sealing rules.

Appendix C. Engineer decision rules
Design has no pass or fail determination. The engineer records an acceptance decision at scoping
and an issue check before anything is sealed.
ACCEPT
-- The work is residential or light commercial and within the engineer's area of competence
-- The scoping record and the required client documents are complete
-- The code and the permitting authority are known
-- The design basis can be written from what is in the file
ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS
-- A site visit is needed, the engineer writes the job list
-- No geotechnical report, the engineer states the assumed soil values or requires a report
-- Existing conditions must be assumed, the engineer lists what the contractor verifies before work
-- Part of the structure is designed by a manufacturer, the engineer states the loads and reviews the
submittal
DECLINE
-- Outside residential and light commercial work, or outside the engineer's area of competence
-- Request to seal work prepared by others not under the engineer's supervision
-- Request to design below the adopted code
-- Open insurance claim or litigation over existing damage, decline, with a referral to an independent
firm
-- Client requests a predetermined conclusion, automatic decline, log verbatim request
ISSUE CHECK (before sealing)
-- Design basis accepted by the client in writing, and the design matches it
-- Code edition and design loads stated on the drawings
254 Engineering Services | 254-DS-001 Structural Design Protocol v1.1 | Page 8
-- Load path complete to the foundation, for gravity, wind, and uplift
-- Every member and connection on the drawings has a calculation or a stated prescriptive basis
-- Drawings checked against the calculations, sheet by sheet
-- Existing conditions assumed, and items designed by others, stated on the drawings
-- Revision number and date on every sheet
REVISE AND REISSUE
-- Permit comment that changes the design
-- Error or omission found after issue
-- Site condition reported by the contractor that differs from the drawings
-- Change requested by the client within the design basis
