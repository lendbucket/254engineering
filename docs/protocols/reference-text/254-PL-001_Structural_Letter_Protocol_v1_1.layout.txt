1. Purpose

This protocol establishes how 254 Engineering Services prepares a structural letter for a permit so
that the engineer can issue a sealed letter to a permitting authority, supported by the documents the
customer supplies and, where existing construction is involved, an evidence package collected by a
field technician under the engineer's supervision.

The engineer signs and seals the letter personally and the permitting authority relies on that opinion.
Every requirement in this protocol exists so that the engineer can reach a determination from the job
file alone.

2. Scope

This protocol applies to residential and light commercial structures where a permitting authority has
asked, in writing, for an engineer's letter on a described structure or element, to issue or close a
permit.

A structural letter for permit is a sealed letter addressed to the permitting authority stating the
engineer's opinion that the described structure or element satisfies the requirement the authority
named.

This protocol does not cover solar structural letters, roof certifications, foundation certifications, or
windstorm certifications, each of which has its own protocol; work that needs drawings or a
specification, which is design or repair specification; or a letter for any reader other than a permitting
authority. The firm does not perform forensic or cause-of-loss investigation under this or any
protocol.

3. Governing requirements

   · The building code adopted by the permitting authority. The coordinator records the authority
       and the code edition at intake. The engineer confirms both.

   · The authority's written request, recorded verbatim. The letter answers that request and nothing
       else.

   · The letter is the engineer's professional opinion on the element described, based on the

4. Roles and responsibilities

Only the Engineer of Record determines whether a job is accepted, what evidence it needs, the
outcome, and the wording of the letter. The field technician and the coordinator relay the engineer's
written determination without interpretation, addition, or estimate of when a letter will issue.

5. Personnel qualifications

Field Technician
   · At least two years of documented experience in residential construction, framing, or structural
       inspection, or equivalent experience the engineer accepts in writing.
   · Completion of the engineer's training on this protocol and Appendix B, with a supervised
       inspection before independent work.
   · Demonstrated ability to read plans and locate items in the field against them.
   · Annual refresher training when the adopted code, this protocol, or Appendix B changes.

Engineer of Record
   · Active Texas P.E. license. A copy, with expiration date, is held in the firm's compliance file.

6. Intake

   · The coordinator opens a job file for each request. The file records the intake answers and
       customer uploads required by Appendix A.

   · The coordinator records what the permitting authority asked for exactly as written, and
       attaches the authority's written request. A request the customer can only describe is not
       accepted.

   · The engineer triages every job within one business day of the order, and records accept,
       accept with conditions, or decline.

   · On acceptance the engineer records whether the job is a site job or a desk job. A site job is
       any job where the letter concerns existing construction. A desk job is one where the letter can
       be answered from drawings and documents alone. Photographs supplied by the customer do
       not replace a technician visit.

   · For a site job, the engineer writes the job list, which is the items the technician captures for that
       element in addition to the standing items of Appendix B.

   · A site job is not dispatched until intake is complete, every upload required by Appendix A has
       been received, and the engineer's job list is in the job file.

7. Scheduling and dispatch

   · The coordinator schedules a site job within two business days of the engineer's job list. The
       engineer may set a different interval for a job in the job file.

   · The technician confirms access and occupancy before travel, and confirms that the element
       can be seen.

   · Where site conditions prevent complete capture, including weather, access, or safety, the
       technician records each item that could not be observed and the reason. No item is estimated,
       assumed, or left blank.

   · A desk job is not dispatched.

8. Field inspection

On a site job the technician completes every item of Appendix B and every item on the engineer's
job list, in the order listed, and follows the photo procedure printed at the head of that checklist.

   · Before starting, the technician makes a rough sketch of the structure, marks North, marks
       where the element is, and photographs the sketch.

   · The technician ticks the item being photographed, photographs the checklist, then takes the
       photographs for that item, and moves to the next item the same way.

   · An item that does not apply is marked with the reason it does not apply.
   · The counts and measurements called for are recorded as counts and measurements, not as

       descriptions.
   · The inspection is visual and non-destructive. The technician does not remove or open any

       finish. An item that is covered up is recorded as concealed.

9. Evidence standard and engineer review

Evidence package
Each site job produces one evidence package in the firm's field application, consisting of the
completed Appendix B checklist, the engineer's job list, the photographs, and the technician's notes.
Each desk job produces a document package consisting of the authority's request and the drawings
and documents reviewed.

   · Every checklist item has at least one wide photograph placing the item on the structure and
       one close photograph showing its condition.

   · Every measurement has the tape in frame.
   · Every connection and every support of the element is photographed individually.
   · Photographs are captured directly in the firm's field application and carry device time, server

       time, the difference between them, and location. Photographs sent by text or messaging app,
       or taken on another device, are not accepted.
   · An observation that cannot be made is recorded as not observed with the reason.
   · A package that does not cover every checklist item and every item on the engineer's job list is
       rejected without review.

Engineer review
   · The engineer reviews each package, performs any calculation the opinion needs, and records
       one of the five determinations in Appendix C: pass, revise, repairs required, site revisit, or
       decline.
   · The engineer's review record identifies the photographs, documents, and calculation relied on
       for the determination. The calculation is kept in the job file.
   · A package that does not allow the engineer to verify a required item is returned for revision or
       a site revisit.

10. Deficiencies and repairs

   · On a repairs required determination, the engineer issues a written repair list identifying each
       item, what governs it, and what is required to close it.

   · Where closing an item needs drawings or a specification, the list says so. That work is a
       separate engagement.

   · Repairs are performed by others. 254 Engineering Services does not perform, supervise, or
       recommend a contractor for the repair work.

   · Corrections are verified on a site revisit to the same evidence standard, and the engineer
       closes each item individually.

   · On a repairs required determination, the engineer also issues a sealed letter stating that the
       element does not satisfy the requirement, with the repair list. No letter stating that the element
       satisfies the requirement is issued with an open repair item.

11. Structural letter

   · On a pass determination, the engineer issues a sealed letter addressed to the permitting
       authority recorded at intake.

   · Where the element satisfies the requirement subject to conditions, the letter says so and states
       each condition in an attached schedule.

   · On a repairs required determination, the engineer issues a sealed letter stating that the
       element does not satisfy the requirement, with the repair list.

   · The letter gives the property, the permit number, the authority's request as written, the element
       described, the adopted code, and the evidence and documents the opinion is based on, with
       the date of inspection for a site job.

   · The letter states the engineer's opinion on the element against the request. It gives no opinion
       on anything the authority did not ask about.

   · The letter states that it does not cover the rest of the structure or any concealed condition, and
       that it reflects condition on the date of inspection only.

   · The letter does not forecast future performance.
   · The letter is not reassigned to another permit or another authority. A new job is required.

12. Exclusions

The following are declined or routed to another service line under its own engagement terms. The
coordinator records the routing in the job file and informs the customer in writing.

   · No written request from the permitting authority. Not accepted until one is supplied.
   · A request that needs drawings, calculations for new work, or a repair method. Routed to the

       design or repair specification line.
   · A request covered by another protocol: solar, roof, foundation, or windstorm. Routed to that

       line.
   · An element that is concealed where the owner will not have it opened. Declined.
   · An open insurance claim or litigation involving the element. Declined, with a referral to an

       independent firm.
   · A customer request for a predetermined conclusion. This is an automatic decline and the

       request is logged verbatim.

13. Records and retention

   · Each job file contains: the intake record and customer uploads, the authority's written request,
       the engineer's acceptance record and job list, the dispatch record, the completed Appendix B
       checklist, all photographs, the technician's notes, the documents reviewed, the engineer's
       calculation, review record and determination, any repair list and its closure records, and the
       issued letter.

   · Job files are retained for ten years from the date of the letter. For a job that produces no letter,
       the ten years runs from the date of the engineer's last recorded determination or closure.

   · Ten years is the firm's floor, reviewed against TBPELS rules.
   · The compliance file holds the engineer's license records, technician qualification and training

       records, and every issued version of this protocol.

14. Protocol maintenance

   · The engineer reviews this protocol annually, and within thirty days of any change to the firm's
       field application.

   · Revisions are issued as a new version number with the effective date and the engineer's
       approval recorded in the document control table.

   · This document follows the firm's house protocol format. The portal's four parts are carried in
       Appendices A through C. Appendix A carries both the intake questions and the customer
       uploads. Appendix D is the reference list.

Appendix C. Engineer decision rules

Applied by the engineer to each package. One determination is recorded per review.

PASS
-- Site job: every checklist item and every job list item present or properly excepted
-- Desk job: every drawing and document the engineer asked for is in the file
-- The authority's request is in the file as written
-- The element, its supports, and its connections are established from the evidence
-- The element satisfies the requirement the authority named, by the adopted code and the
engineer's calculation where one is needed
-- No damage or alteration that affects the opinion

                        254 Engineering Services | 254-PL-001 Structural Letter Protocol v1.1 | Page 8
-- Where the opinion depends on a condition, the letter states the condition in an attached schedule

REVISE
-- Any required photo missing, blurry, or missing its tape
-- A support or connection with no photo
-- A job list item not completed
-- A drawing or document the engineer asked for not supplied
-- Counts or measurements inconsistent with photos

REPAIRS REQUIRED (sealed letter states does not satisfy, repair list issued)
-- The element does not satisfy the requirement the authority named
-- A support or connection is missing, undersized, or not installed to its manufacturer's instructions
-- Damage or alteration that affects the element
-- A letter stating that the element satisfies the requirement proceeds only after repairs are verified
on revisit

SITE REVISIT (technician returns with an engineer-specified capture list)
-- A concealed support or connection governs the result, return after the owner's contractor opens it
and photograph it
-- The first visit showed something the job list did not cover, return with an added list
-- Conflicting evidence (plans vs. photos vs. measurements), return for the specific items that settle it
-- Repairs required, return after repairs to verify each item on the repair list

DECLINE
-- No written request from the permitting authority
-- Request needs drawings or a repair method, route to design or repair specification line
-- Request covered by another protocol, route to that line
-- Concealed element and the owner will not have it opened
-- Open insurance claim or litigation, decline, with a referral to an independent firm
-- Customer requests a predetermined conclusion, automatic decline, log verbatim request
