## The short answer

The Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) windstorm inspection program covers all of 14 coastal counties, from Cameron at the Mexican border to Jefferson at the Louisiana line, and five cities east of State Highway 146 in Harris County. A building there needs a TDI certificate of compliance to be eligible for wind and hail coverage through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA).

## Which counties are in the program?

TDI calls them the first tier counties: Aransas, Brazoria, Calhoun, Cameron, Chambers, Galveston, Jefferson, Kenedy, Kleberg, Matagorda, Nueces, Refugio, San Patricio and Willacy. TDI's page on the 2024 codes says that, except for Harris County, the entire county of each one is part of the windstorm program, so there is no inland edge inside these 14 where the program stops.

Harris County is the exception. Only structures east of State Highway 146 and inside the city limits of La Porte, Morgan's Point, Pasadena, Seabrook and Shoreacres are in it. A house in Houston, or in an unincorporated part of the county east of 146, is outside the program.

## What does TDI require?

For work on a building in these areas, the order is fixed:

1. Form WPI-1, the application for a certificate of compliance, goes to TDI before construction starts. TDI's general questions page notes that a building permit office can ask for proof of that enrollment.
2. A TDI inspector or an appointed Texas licensed professional engineer inspects the work while it is in progress. TDI's inspection page says its own inspectors inspect during the work, not before or after it.
3. The engineer files the WPI-2 inspection verification forms with TDI.
4. TDI issues the WPI-8 certificate of compliance once every required inspection is approved.

Work that was finished without those inspections takes the completed construction route instead: a signed WPI-2E application with a sealed post construction inspection report, or a sealed design, and TDI issues a WPI-8-E. Any engineer licensed by the Texas Board of Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors may inspect completed construction; ongoing construction needs a TDI appointed inspector.

Since April 1, 2026, a new application must be certified to the 2024 International Residential Code or the 2024 International Building Code. TDI says wind speed is now worked out for each structure from its location, using the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool, and that most of the catastrophe area now sits inside the windborne debris region.

## The old wind zones, county by county

Under the 2006 codes with the Texas revisions, which TDI applied from January 1, 2008 to August 31, 2020, each county was split into up to three zones with a set design wind speed (3 second gust): Seaward at 130 mph, Inland I at 120 mph and Inland II at 110 mph. These zones no longer set the wind speed for new work. They still matter when reading a certificate or a set of drawings from those years, because the zone is the design wind speed the building was certified to.

A few rules on those pages decide cases the table cannot:

- **A city split by the Inland I and Inland II line** was treated as Inland II throughout, with named exceptions.
- **Corpus Christi, Angleton and Brownsville** were the exceptions. Each was held to Inland I across its whole city limits, even where the line crossed it.
- **The dividing line follows roads, not distance.** It runs up U.S. Highway 77 from the western city limits of Brownsville through Willacy, Kenedy and Kleberg, past Corpus Christi, and north through Sinton and Refugio County. Through Matagorda and Brazoria it follows F.M. 616, State Highway 35 and State Highway 6. In Jefferson County it follows Interstate 10 and the southeastern city limits of Beaumont.
- **Harris County's five cities** were all Inland I, for work done on or after June 1, 2008.

TDI marks each map as informational and not suitable for legal, engineering or surveying purposes. The map page for the county, and the county map image on it, is where a specific address is checked.

## How do I look up a certificate?

Where to look depends on who issued it:

- **WPI-8 and WPI-8-E:** TDI issues both, and every certificate of compliance TDI has issued for completed and ongoing construction is published in its online windstorm search. TDI's general questions page also gives the Windstorm Inspections Program telephone line, 800-248-6032, option 3, for older records.
- **WPI-8-C:** TWIA issued these for completed construction on applications it accepted from January 1, 2017 to May 31, 2020, and TWIA's lookup searches those only. TWIA's page sends anything outside that window to the TDI database.

Since June 1, 2020, TDI issues every certificate of compliance. The full walk through of both searches, including what to try when an address returns nothing, is on [the windstorm certificate lookup guide](/insights/texas-windstorm-certificate-lookup).

## Does every building in these counties need a certificate?

Not always to be insured. TWIA's certification page says a certificate must be provided before a property is eligible for TWIA coverage, and it names one exception: residential property built from 1988 to June 18, 2009 without a certificate may still be eligible with a 15 percent surcharge. New structures, additions and other improvements still need certifying.

## FAQ (for the FAQPage, matching the visible questions)

**Is all of Nueces County in the windstorm program?**
Yes. TDI says that, except for Harris County, the entire county of each designated county is in the windstorm program, so every address in Nueces County is inside it.

**Which parts of Harris County need a windstorm certificate?**
Only structures east of State Highway 146 and inside the city limits of La Porte, Morgan's Point, Pasadena, Seabrook and Shoreacres.

**What were the Seaward, Inland I and Inland II zones?**
The wind zones under the 2006 codes with the Texas revisions, applied from 2008 to 2020: Seaward at 130 mph, Inland I at 120 mph and Inland II at 110 mph, each a 3 second gust design wind speed. New work since April 1, 2026 is certified to the 2024 codes with wind speed set for each structure.

**Where do I look up a WPI-8-C?**
On TWIA's lookup, which covers only WPI-8-C certificates on applications from January 1, 2017 to May 31, 2020. WPI-8 and WPI-8-E certificates are in TDI's online search.
